## PART I — INTERPRETATION & FOUNDATIONAL DEFINITIONS

### ARTICLE 1 — Definitions

- “Club” means GREYMEN’s CLUB.
- “Institution” means ADYPU and the relevant School/departmental authority governing the Club, as applicable.
- “Faculty Coordinator” means a faculty member formally designated to coordinate the Club with the Institution.
- “Faculty Advisor” means a faculty member recognized for academic, technical or mentoring support to the Club.
- “General Body” means the body of members entitled to participate in Club-wide membership decisions under this Charter.
- “Executive Board” means the principal student governing body established under Part V.
- “General Council” means the administrative, communications, editorial, membership and community body chaired by the General Secretary.
- “Technical Directorate” means the technical functions led by the CTO and its divisional leads.
- “Security Activity” means any activity involving security testing, defensive security, offensive-security education, vulnerability analysis, CTF infrastructure or related technical activity.
- “Authorized Activity” means an activity for which the person has explicit permission, within a defined scope, to access or test the relevant system, information or environment.
- “Officer” means a student appointed or elected to a formal Club office.
- “Standing body” means a continuing body established by this Charter rather than for one specific event or project.
- “Project Team” or “Event Team” means a temporary group constituted for a defined purpose and period.
- “Good standing” means a member who is eligible to participate and is not under a sanction that restricts the relevant participation or office.
### ARTICLE 2 — Interpretation and Hierarchy

2.1 This Charter shall be interpreted to support the Club’s stated purpose while maintaining student leadership, institutional accountability, technical competence, responsible conduct and continuity.

2.2 Where a provision of this Charter conflicts with applicable law or binding institutional policy, the higher authority shall prevail to the extent of the conflict.

2.3 The Charter establishes the Club’s governance, management, operating, technical, disciplinary and continuity requirements in one comprehensive governing document. Detailed procedures, forms, checklists and technical standards may be maintained within the relevant Articles, Schedules or Appendices of this Charter and shall not contradict this Charter.

2.4 Where the wording of a provision is reasonably open to more than one interpretation, the interpretation that best preserves institutional compliance, due process, least privilege, member participation and continuity should be preferred.

|Core governance rule No office, committee, project team or individual member may exercise authority that this Charter does not grant or that has not been validly delegated under it.|

## PART II — IDENTITY, PURPOSE & PRINCIPLES

### ARTICLE 3 — Name, Status and Identity

3.1 The organization shall be known as GREYMEN’s CLUB.

3.2 GREYMEN’s CLUB is a student technology organization operating within the institutional framework of ADYPU and the School of Engineering, subject to applicable institutional requirements.

3.3 The Club’s central technical identity is cybersecurity. The Club also supports related and intersecting areas including AI/ML, software and web development, cloud, data, systems, research and emerging technologies.

3.4 The Club is interdisciplinary. A member’s academic program does not, by itself, limit the technical areas in which that member may participate, subject to competence, authorization and the requirements of a particular activity.

### ARTICLE 4 — Purpose and Objectives

4.1 The purpose of GREYMEN’s CLUB is to create a continuous interdisciplinary student technology community with cybersecurity at its core, where students connect through shared interests, skills and specializations to discuss, debate, explore, build, compete, research, collaborate and grow across cybersecurity, AI/ML, software and web development, cloud, data and emerging technologies.

4.2 The Club shall promote learning through practical work, technical discussion, responsible experimentation, projects, research, competitions and peer collaboration.

4.3 The Club shall encourage members to develop technical competence together with professional judgment, ethics, documentation discipline, communication and responsible use of technology.

4.4 The Club shall maintain a work-centric culture. Events, certificates and promotional activity are supporting mechanisms rather than the primary purpose of membership.

### ARTICLE 5 — Foundational Principles

• Student-led governance within institutional oversight.
• Cybersecurity as the Club’s core technical identity.
• Interdisciplinary participation without treating academic specialization as a boundary on technical interest.
• Authorized and responsible use of technology.
• Competence-based technical responsibility and least-privilege access.
• Proportionate discipline, procedural fairness and the right to respond.
• Documentation, continuity and responsible handover.
• Non-discriminatory access to membership and participation, subject to applicable institutional rules.
• Protection of people, systems, information and institutional resources.
• Continuous improvement through periodic review.
## PART III — MEMBERSHIP & GENERAL BODY

### ARTICLE 6 — Membership Categories

|Category|Description|Core rights|
|---|---|---|
|General Member|Student admitted to the Club under the membership process.|Participate in open Club activities and member programs subject to rules.|
|Active Member|General Member meeting the participation/engagement requirements defined by the Club.|May receive voting eligibility and eligibility for specified opportunities, subject to this Charter.|
|Officer|Member holding a formal elected or appointed office.|Exercises office-specific authority while in good standing.|
|Project / Specialized Team Member|Member temporarily assigned to a project, research, CTF or other specialized team.|Access and authority limited to the assigned scope.|
|Alumni / Former Member|Former member recognized for continuing association with the Club.|Advisory or honorary participation as permitted; no ordinary voting authority unless expressly granted.|

### ARTICLE 7 — Admission, Rights and Responsibilities

7.1 Membership admission shall be administered through the process approved by the Executive Board and General Secretary, consistent with institutional requirements.

7.2 Members shall have reasonable access to Club activities appropriate to their membership status and the nature of the activity.

7.3 Members shall comply with this Charter, approved Club rules, event or project instructions, applicable institutional requirements and any lawful conditions attached to technical access.

7.4 Members shall protect Club credentials, systems, information and property entrusted to them.

7.5 Membership does not itself grant authority to access or test any information system or service.

7.6 Members may raise concerns, make good-faith reports and participate in governance processes available to their membership category.

### ARTICLE 8 — Active Membership and Voting Eligibility

8.1 The Club may define reasonable participation criteria for Active Member status, such as participation in Club activities, projects, meetings, research, competitions or other meaningful contribution.

8.2 Participation criteria shall be published sufficiently in advance of any election or vote for which Active Member status affects eligibility.

8.3 Active Member status shall not be used arbitrarily to exclude members from participation or to engineer election outcomes.

### ARTICLE 9 — General Body

9.1 The General Body consists of members entitled to vote under this Charter.

9.2 The General Body shall elect the offices designated as elected offices and shall consider constitutional amendments and other matters reserved to it.

9.3 The General Body shall not ordinarily intervene in day-to-day technical, administrative or event decisions delegated to the Executive Board or its subordinate bodies.

9.4 An Annual General Meeting shall provide members with a formal opportunity to receive organizational, financial and activity updates and to raise questions within the scope of the meeting.

## PART V — STUDENT EXECUTIVE GOVERNANCE

### ARTICLE 13 — Executive Board

13.1 The Executive Board is the principal student governing body of GREYMEN’s CLUB.

13.2 The Executive Board shall consist of the following core offices: President; Vice President; General Secretary; Treasurer; CTO / Technical Director; Cybersecurity Lead; Research & Projects Director.

13.3 The Executive Board may invite divisional leads, project leads, Faculty Advisors or other members to meetings when their expertise is relevant. Invitees may participate in discussion but do not obtain a vote solely by invitation.

### ARTICLE 14 — President

• Provides overall student leadership and represents the Club within authorized institutional and external settings.
• Chairs Executive Board meetings and coordinates implementation of strategic decisions.
• Works with Faculty Coordinators on institutional matters.
• Promotes continuity, accountability and compliance with this Charter.
• May exercise tie-breaking authority where expressly provided and where no conflict of interest exists.
• Shall not override specialized technical or security controls merely by virtue of office.
### ARTICLE 15 — Vice President

• Supports the President in executive coordination and acts in the President’s absence as provided by this Charter.
• Coordinates cross-directorate initiatives and follow-up of major Executive Board decisions.
• Helps resolve operational dependencies between the General Council and technical/research functions.
• May coordinate special committees or task forces established by the Executive Board.
### ARTICLE 16 — General Secretary

• Is the principal administrative officer of the Club and chairs the General Council.
• Maintains the official governance record, including meeting minutes, resolutions, officer records and amendments.
• Coordinates membership administration and organizational notices.
• Maintains handover and continuity records in coordination with outgoing and incoming officers.
• Ensures that subordinate operational documents are consistent with this Charter.
### ARTICLE 17 — Treasurer

• Maintains student-side financial records and budget tracking.
• Coordinates expenditure documentation and approved reimbursement or procurement processes.
• Maintains records of sponsorships, contributions and financial commitments where permitted.
• Works with Faculty Coordinators and institutional finance processes where required.
• May not independently commit institutional funds or sign binding financial agreements without required authorization.
### ARTICLE 18 — CTO / Technical Director

• Leads the Club’s overall technical architecture and technical standards.
• Coordinates technical divisions, shared development practices and technical infrastructure.
• Oversees the reliability, maintainability and continuity of Club-controlled technical systems.
• Coordinates with the Cybersecurity Lead on security-sensitive infrastructure and access.
• Ensures that project and event technical resources have an identified owner and handover path.
### ARTICLE 19 — Cybersecurity Lead

• Leads cybersecurity activities and security governance within the Club’s mandate.
• Oversees security labs, authorized security testing, responsible disclosure practices and cybersecurity education.
• Defines or approves security controls for Club-managed security activities.
• Coordinates with the CTO and Infrastructure / Systems Lead on security-sensitive systems.
• May recommend or initiate temporary access restrictions when reasonably necessary to contain a security risk, subject to prompt reporting and review.
• Guides the CTF & Competitive Security Lead.
### ARTICLE 20 — Research & Projects Director

• Coordinates research programs and long-running technical projects.
• Facilitates project formation, project leads, milestones and internal review.
• Supports responsible research practices, attribution and publication quality.
• Coordinates interdisciplinary teams and mentoring opportunities.
• Maintains a portfolio-level view of ongoing projects without micromanaging technical implementation.

### ARTICLE 26 — Documentation Lead

## PART VIII — CYBERSECURITY, RESPONSIBLE TECHNOLOGY & TECHNICAL ACCESS

### ARTICLE 35 — Authorized Activity

|Non-negotiable rule Club membership is not permission to access, scan, exploit, modify, disrupt, extract data from or otherwise interact with a system. Permission must exist for the specific activity and scope.|
|---|

35.1 No member or officer may access, test, scan, exploit, modify, disrupt, extract or otherwise interact with a computer system, network, application, account, device, service or information resource unless the activity is explicitly authorized and remains within the authorized scope.

35.2 Authorized environments may include Club-owned laboratories, purpose-built CTF platforms, systems expressly provided for testing, or systems for which written or otherwise verifiable permission exists.

35.3 Authorization does not extend beyond its stated scope, target, time period, technique, data boundary or purpose.

### ARTICLE 36 — Responsible Disclosure

• Members who identify a vulnerability through Club activity shall preserve relevant evidence and follow the Club’s authorized disclosure process.
• Sensitive technical details shall not be publicly released merely because a member discovered them.
• Where a vulnerability concerns a third party or institutional resource, disclosure shall be coordinated through the appropriate authority and applicable institutional requirements.
### ARTICLE 37 — Technical Access and Least Privilege

• Technical access shall be granted according to role, need and assigned scope.
• Privileged access shall be limited to people who require it for a defined responsibility.
• Credentials and secrets shall be protected and should not be shared unnecessarily.
• Access shall be reviewed and revoked when a role ends, access is no longer needed, or a security incident reasonably requires restriction.
• Club-controlled accounts and technical resources belong to the Club or the institution as applicable, not to the individual officer who created or administered them.
### ARTICLE 38 — Security Incident Containment

38.1 Where a Club-controlled system or account may be compromised, the Cybersecurity Lead and/or Infrastructure / Systems Lead may take immediate protective measures within their authority, including disabling access, revoking credentials, isolating systems or preserving logs.

38.2 Interim containment is a protective action and shall not, by itself, be treated as a final finding of misconduct.

38.3 Material incidents shall be promptly escalated to the CTO, President and, where appropriate, a Faculty Coordinator.

### ARTICLE 39 — Technical Infrastructure Ownership

39.1 Club repositories, domains, servers, cloud resources, CTF infrastructure, shared services and official technical accounts shall be administered as organizational resources.

39.2 Wherever practical, organizational ownership rather than personal ownership shall be used for critical systems.

39.3 Every critical technical resource shall have an identified owner, at least one backup custodian where practical, and a handover path.

## PART IX — ELECTIONS, APPOINTMENTS, TERMS & SUCCESSION

### ARTICLE 40 — Elected Offices

40.1 The following offices shall ordinarily be elected by the eligible General Body: President, Vice President, General Secretary and Treasurer.

40.2 The Constitution may be amended in future to move additional offices to direct election, but technical competence should remain a required eligibility factor for specialized offices.

### ARTICLE 41 — Appointed / Selected Offices

41.1 The CTO, Cybersecurity Lead, Research & Projects Director, technical divisional leads and General Council leads may be selected through a documented nomination and appointment process.

41.2 Appointments should consider competence, reliability, contribution, conduct, availability and the requirements of the position.

41.3 No appointment may be made for the purpose of evading an applicable disciplinary restriction.

### ARTICLE 42 — Nominations and Selection

42.1 The Club may establish a nomination or selection panel for appointed offices.

42.2 A person involved in a material conflict of interest shall not control the final decision concerning their own selection, a close personal associate’s selection, or a disputed disciplinary appointment.

### ARTICLE 43 — Eligibility for Office

• Must be a member in good standing and satisfy any applicable institutional eligibility requirement.
• Must be able to perform the duties of the office.
• Specialized positions may require demonstrated technical competence or relevant prior contribution.
• A person under a sanction that prohibits holding office is not eligible until reinstated.
### ARTICLE 44 — Term of Office

44.1 The ordinary term shall be one academic year unless institutional rules or a ratified resolution specify otherwise.

44.2 Officers may be re-elected or reappointed where permitted, but continuation shall not be automatic merely because a replacement has not yet been identified.

### ARTICLE 45 — Vacancies and Succession

|Office / situation|Interim arrangement|
|---|---|
|President vacant|Vice President acts, followed by the prescribed election or succession process.|
|Vice President vacant|President may nominate an interim officer subject to the appointment process and next General Body/election requirement where applicable.|
|General Secretary vacant|Executive Board appoints an interim officer to preserve records and General Council continuity.|
|Technical director vacant|Relevant Executive Board authority appoints an interim lead with necessary competence.|
|Any office vacant|The Club shall prioritize continuity, documented handover and timely permanent replacement.|

### ARTICLE 46 — Handover and Continuity

• Every outgoing officer shall provide a structured handover covering current work, pending matters, resources, contacts, documentation, credentials or access transition through approved procedures, and known risks.
• Critical technical resources shall not depend on one individual’s personal account.
• The General Secretary shall maintain an organizational continuity record, while sensitive technical information shall remain restricted to authorized personnel.
## PART X — MEETINGS, QUORUM & DECISION-MAKING

### ARTICLE 47 — Meetings of the Executive Board

47.1 The Executive Board shall meet as reasonably required to conduct Club governance and should maintain a regular cadence during active academic periods.

47.2 Reasonable notice and an agenda should be provided for ordinary meetings. Emergency meetings may be called with shorter notice when necessary.

### ARTICLE 48 — General Council Meetings

48.1 The General Secretary shall convene General Council meetings as required for administrative coordination.

48.2 Each portfolio shall report material progress, pending tasks, issues and decisions requiring escalation.

### ARTICLE 49 — General Body Meetings and Annual General Meeting

49.1 The Annual General Meeting shall include, as appropriate, a review of Club activity, leadership, membership, finances and future priorities.

49.2 Special General Body meetings may be called when required by this Charter or when requested by the required proportion of eligible voting members.

### ARTICLE 50 — Quorum

50.1 Executive Board quorum shall be a simple majority of its voting members unless institutional rules require a different standard.

50.2 General Council quorum shall be a simple majority of its standing voting participants for matters requiring a Council decision.

50.3 General Body quorum shall be defined in the ratified election procedures and shall be set high enough to provide legitimacy while remaining practical for a student organization.

### ARTICLE 51 — Decision-Making and Voting

51.1 Ordinary decisions of a body shall be determined by simple majority of votes cast, provided quorum exists and this Charter does not require a higher threshold.

51.2 Abstentions shall not ordinarily count as affirmative or negative votes.

51.3 Constitutional amendments, dissolution and removal of senior elected officers may require higher thresholds defined in the relevant provisions.

51.4 Specialized technical decisions should ordinarily be made by the competent office within its mandate rather than by non-technical majority vote.

### ARTICLE 53 — Conflict Resolution

53.1 Operational disagreements should first be addressed by the relevant lead, then escalated to the relevant director or General Secretary/VP, and then to the President where necessary.

53.2 A dispute involving the President, or a matter in which impartiality is reasonably in question, shall be escalated to the Faculty Coordinators or another appropriate institutional authority.

53.3 A security-sensitive dispute shall preserve the authority of the Cybersecurity Lead to maintain reasonable security controls pending resolution.

## PART XI — PROJECTS, EVENTS, COMPETITIONS & COMMITTEES

### ARTICLE 54 — Projects

54.1 Projects should have a defined objective, lead, members, scope, expected outputs, risk profile and closure/review condition.

54.2 Projects involving security testing, sensitive data, privileged infrastructure or external systems shall undergo the necessary authorization and security review before execution.

### ARTICLE 55 — Events and Activities

55.1 Club events may include cybersecurity workshops, technical discussions, CTFs, security debates and case discussions, technical workshops, project showcases, research sessions, hackathons and competitions, awareness activities and expert interactions.

55.2 Events should have a named coordinator and, where relevant, separate technical, operations, communications and documentation responsibilities.

55.3 Event-specific responsibilities may be assigned without creating permanent constitutional offices.

### ARTICLE 56 — Temporary Committees and Task Forces

56.1 The Executive Board may establish a temporary committee, task force, event team, project team or competition team by resolution.

56.2 The creating resolution should identify the purpose, lead, membership, authority, expected output and conclusion/termination condition.

56.3 Temporary bodies do not possess authority beyond the scope granted to them.

### ARTICLE 57 — Financial Governance

57.1 Club finances shall be administered in accordance with applicable institutional finance rules.

57.2 The Treasurer shall maintain accurate records of approved financial activity and supporting documentation.

57.3 Sponsorships, fundraising and external financial arrangements must follow institutional requirements and shall not create undisclosed obligations for the Club or Institution.

### ARTICLE 58 — Expenditure and Approval

58.1 Expenditure should be approved before commitment wherever practicable.

58.2 Emergency expenditure may be handled under an institutional emergency process, followed by prompt documentation.

58.3 No officer may approve a payment to themselves without an independent review or required institutional procedure.

59.1 Club-controlled physical and digital assets shall be tracked appropriately.

59.2 Assets may include hardware, security-lab equipment, books, domains, hosting accounts, repositories, cloud resources, software services and other organizational resources.

59.3 Organizational assets shall remain with the Club or Institution as applicable when an officer leaves office.

## PART XIII — INFORMATION, DATA, INTELLECTUAL PROPERTY & REPRESENTATION

### ARTICLE 60 — Information and Privacy

60.1 Members shall handle personal information, credentials, private communications and sensitive Club data only for legitimate Club purposes and within authorized access.

60.2 Sensitive information shall be shared only with persons who need it for a defined responsibility.

### ARTICLE 61 — Research and Intellectual Contribution

61.1 The Club shall encourage attribution, accurate reporting, responsible authorship and avoidance of plagiarism.

61.2 Project ownership, intellectual property and publication rights shall be determined in accordance with applicable institutional rules and any valid project-specific agreement.

61.3 The Club shall not automatically claim ownership of every work created by every member merely because the work was created by a member.

### ARTICLE 62 — Official Representation and Branding

62.1 Only authorized persons may make official statements on behalf of GREYMEN’s CLUB or commit the Club to an external position.

62.2 Use of the Club name, logo, channels, domain, official account or other identity shall follow approved branding and communication rules.

62.3 Members shall not present personal opinions as official Club positions without authorization.

### ARTICLE 63 — External Partnerships and Competitions

63.1 The Club may collaborate with companies, communities, colleges, conferences and other organizations subject to applicable institutional procedures.

63.2 External activities involving contracts, money, data-sharing, institutional branding or legal commitments shall follow the required approval process.

## PART XIV — CONDUCT, OFFENCES & DISCIPLINE

### ARTICLE 64 — Standards of Conduct

• Members shall act with reasonable professionalism, respect and integrity in Club activities and official channels.
• Members shall not misuse Club authority, resources, identity, access or information.
• Members shall comply with applicable safety, institutional and cybersecurity requirements.
• Members shall not retaliate against a person who makes a good-faith report or participates in a review.
### ARTICLE 65 — Offence Categories

|Level|General character|Illustrative examples|Typical response range|
|---|---|---|---|
|Level I — Minor|Low-impact conduct or administrative breach.|Disruption, minor procedure breach, first low-level misuse of a Club channel.|Advisory or verbal warning; correction or training.|
|Level II — Moderate|Meaningful misconduct, repetition or negligence.|Repeated breach, misrepresentation, improper disclosure of non-public information, misuse of office.|Written warning, probation, restriction, removal from activity or role.|
|Level III — Major|Serious misconduct with substantial harm or risk.|Financial misconduct, serious harassment, deliberate access-control circumvention, deliberate compromise of Club systems.|Suspension, role removal, termination of access/membership, institutional referral.|
|Level IV — Critical|Conduct creating severe security, safety, legal or institutional risk.|Unauthorized attack on third-party systems, credential theft, malware deployment, destructive access, serious fraud.|Immediate protective measures plus suspension/termination and referral as appropriate.|

### ARTICLE 66 — Cybersecurity and Technical Offences

• Unauthorized access or attempted unauthorized access.
• Exceeding the authorized scope of a security activity.
• Using Club infrastructure to attack or scan external targets without authorization.
• Credential theft, deliberate credential sharing, or misuse of privileged access.
• Deployment of malware or destructive tooling against unauthorized systems.
• Intentional extraction, retention or disclosure of sensitive information without authorization.
• Intentional circumvention of technical controls for unauthorized access or privilege.
• Concealment or destruction of logs or other evidence relating to a security incident.
• Knowingly misrepresenting an activity as a Club-authorized exercise when it is not authorized.
### ARTICLE 67 — Other Serious Offences

• Fraud, theft or deliberate misappropriation of Club resources.
• Falsification of official Club records, participation, research or financial documentation.
• Plagiarism or deliberate misrepresentation of another person’s work.
• Harassment, threats, intimidation or serious discriminatory misconduct.
• Abuse of office for personal advantage or retaliation against members.
• Unauthorized disclosure of confidential member, project, security or institutional information.
• Interference with an investigation, destruction of evidence or retaliation against a complainant or witness.
### ARTICLE 68 — Aggravating and Mitigating Factors

• Intentional conduct may warrant a stronger sanction than an accidental act.
• Actual harm, potential harm, financial impact, security impact and institutional consequences may increase severity.
• Prompt self-reporting, cooperation, remediation and genuine corrective action may be considered mitigating factors.
• Concealment, retaliation, repeated misconduct or abuse of privileged authority may aggravate the matter.
• The member’s responsibility level and access privileges may be considered when assessing seriousness.
### ARTICLE 69 — Warnings and Sanctions

|Sanction|Purpose|
|---|---|
|Advisory warning|Informal correction and clarification of expectations.|
|Written warning|Formal notice of misconduct and required correction.|
|Final warning|Formal warning that further misconduct may lead to stronger sanctions.|
|Probation|Continued membership or participation subject to defined conditions.|
|Access suspension|Temporary restriction of technical, facility or account access.|
|Removal from activity / project / team|Ends participation in a defined activity without necessarily ending membership.|
|Removal from office|Ends a formal leadership position while membership may continue.|
|Membership suspension|Temporary loss of Club participation privileges.|
|Membership termination|Ends Club membership after the applicable process.|
|Institutional / external referral|Escalation to the appropriate authority where required or appropriate.|

### ARTICLE 70 — Proportionate Discipline

70.1 Sanctions shall be proportionate to the nature, severity, intent, impact, repetition and circumstances of the violation.

70.2 The Club may combine sanctions where appropriate, such as access suspension together with a warning, or role removal together with continuing membership.

70.3 A temporary protective restriction shall not automatically determine the final disciplinary outcome.

### ARTICLE 71 — Officer Accountability

71.1 An officer may be subject to enhanced accountability where misconduct involves abuse of office, privileged access, Club funds, confidential information or authority entrusted to the officer.

71.2 Removal from office is distinct from termination of membership.

## PART XV — COMPLAINTS, INVESTIGATION & APPEALS

### ARTICLE 72 — Reporting and Complaints

72.1 Complaints may be submitted to the General Secretary, President, relevant senior officer or a Faculty Coordinator as appropriate to the nature of the matter.

72.2 Where the complaint concerns one of the ordinary reporting recipients, the complainant may report directly to another designated authority so that no person is required to decide their own case.

72.3 Good-faith reporting shall not itself constitute misconduct merely because the allegation is not ultimately substantiated.

### ARTICLE 73 — Preliminary Assessment

73.1 A preliminary assessment shall determine whether the matter appears to fall within Club jurisdiction, whether immediate protective action is required, and whether the matter should be referred to the Institution.

### ARTICLE 74 — Interim Protective Measures

• Temporary suspension of technical access.
• Temporary removal from a project, competition or event.
• Temporary restrictions on use of Club systems or credentials.
• Preservation of records or logs.
• Other proportionate measures reasonably necessary to protect people, systems or information.
### ARTICLE 75 — Review and Opportunity to Respond

75.1 Before a serious final sanction is imposed, the person concerned shall ordinarily receive notice of the allegation and a reasonable opportunity to respond, subject to institutional rules and any necessary interim protection.

75.2 The review should consider relevant evidence and should avoid unnecessary disclosure of sensitive information.

### ARTICLE 76 — Conflict of Interest

76.1 A person shall not be the sole decision-maker on a disciplinary matter in which they are directly involved or have a material conflict of interest.

### ARTICLE 77 — Decision

77.1 A disciplinary decision should identify, to the extent appropriate, the finding, applicable rule, sanction and effective date.

77.2 Decisions should be documented confidentially and retained according to the Club’s records practice and institutional requirements.

### ARTICLE 78 — Appeals

78.1 A member may appeal a serious disciplinary decision on grounds including material procedural unfairness, relevant new evidence, a significant conflict of interest or a sanction that is clearly disproportionate.

78.2 Appeals should be reviewed by a different authorized person or panel from the original decision-maker wherever practicable.

### ARTICLE 79 — Confidentiality and Referral

79.1 Disciplinary information shall be shared only with persons who reasonably need it for governance, safeguarding, institutional compliance, legal obligations or the review itself.

79.2 Confidentiality shall not prevent required reporting to the Institution or another competent authority.

## PART XVI — RECORDS, DOCUMENTATION & ORGANIZATIONAL CONTINUITY

### ARTICLE 80 — Official Records

• Constitution, amendments and ratification records.
• Executive Board and General Council minutes and resolutions.
• Officer appointment/election records.
• Membership records.
• Annual activity and financial records.
• Asset and infrastructure ownership records.
• Project and event records.
• Disciplinary records subject to access restrictions.
### ARTICLE 81 — Documentation Responsibilities

81.1 The General Secretary is the custodian of the overall governance record.

81.2 The Documentation Lead maintains operational documentation and supports the creation of annual reports and archives.

81.3 The CTO, Cybersecurity Lead and Infrastructure / Systems Lead maintain technical documentation appropriate to their responsibilities, with security-sensitive information restricted appropriately.

### ARTICLE 82 — Annual Governance Review

82.1 The Club shall review this Charter at least once each academic year or whenever a major structural, institutional or operational change makes review necessary.

82.2 The review should consider whether offices remain useful, whether authority remains clear, whether disciplinary provisions remain workable, and whether technical access and continuity controls remain adequate.

## PART XVII — AMENDMENTS, INTERPRETATION & DISSOLUTION

### ARTICLE 83 — Amendments

83.1 Amendments shall be proposed in writing with sufficient notice to eligible members before a vote.

83.2 Constitutional amendments should require a higher approval threshold than ordinary operational decisions and shall be subject to institutional approval where required.

83.3 Each approved amendment shall be recorded in the amendment history with its date, description and approving authority.

### ARTICLE 84 — Subordinate Rules and Operating Documents

84.1 Detailed procedures, technical standards, event rules, CTF rules, forms and other operating requirements shall be established, recorded and maintained within this Constitution & Governance Charter, including its schedules and appendices.

84.2 No subordinate document may contradict this Charter or applicable institutional requirements.

84.3 Where this Charter grants a technical or administrative office discretion to establish procedures, those procedures should be documented and made available to the people who are expected to follow them.

### ARTICLE 85 — Interpretive Authority

85.1 Questions concerning the meaning or application of this Charter shall initially be considered by the Executive Board, subject to Faculty Coordinator and institutional oversight where relevant.

85.2 An interpretation shall not be used to create a new permanent office, financial authority or disciplinary power that the Charter does not reasonably permit; such structural changes require formal amendment.

### ARTICLE 86 — Dissolution

86.1 Dissolution of GREYMEN’s CLUB shall require the institutional process and voting threshold applicable to the Club.

86.2 Upon dissolution, Club-controlled assets, funds, records, accounts, infrastructure and intellectual materials shall be transferred, archived or disposed of according to applicable institutional requirements rather than distributed informally among individual members.
